## Judgment

The **31.6% arithmetic comparison is reproducible**, but the causal wording "the pilot reduced penalty by 31.6%" is too strong. The defensible claim is: on 29 May, the AI-assisted accepted schedule recorded Rs453,085.88 DSM penalty versus Rs662,444.09 on the selected 28 May conventional day, 31.604% lower, with absolute generation gap 25.391% lower. This is useful matched-day evidence, not a causal effect estimate. Confidence: high on arithmetic; low-to-medium on causality.

## Rejected Hypotheses and Causal Limits

- **Arithmetic/reporting error:** rejected; the two headline percentages reproduce to rounding from block data and match the impact note.
- **The comparison is worthless:** rejected; near-matched observed wind/stow conditions plus the within-day base-versus-submitted check provide directional evidence.
- **31.6% is the pilot's causal effect:** rejected. It is one treated day versus one chosen day, with no randomization. Actual generation profiles, forecast errors, gate availability, operator discretion, and settlement rules may differ. The effect of AI versus a human/QCA revision remains unidentified.

## Next Measurement

Pre-register the next eligible high-wind events and randomly alternate pilot-versus-base submission at the decision deadline (or use a matched stepped-wedge if randomization is unsafe). Persist both frozen candidate schedules, the forecast available at decision time, gate/acceptance timestamps, stow/availability/curtailment, meter actuals, DSM bands, and final settlement. Estimate paired block-level intent-to-treat effects across multiple events, not a selected daily pair.

## Recommendation

Continue only in a reversible, human-approved pilot/shadow mode; do not advertise 31.6% or expand on this sample. First reconcile the block calculation to the final settlement under the full regulation. Expand only after repeated prospective comparisons show penalty improvement without lost energy value or operational guardrail breaches.
